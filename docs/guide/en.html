1. What Leyline does with your files

Three sentences, and the rest follows from them.

Your files are never modified. A RAW is read, never rewritten. No setting, no rating, no keyword is written into your photographs. What you do in Leyline lives in the library, beside them.

The library references; it does not contain. A library is a folder holding a catalog (a SQLite database), previews, and nothing else. At import you choose: copy into the library, and the files are filed under its Photos/ folder; or leave them where they are, and the catalog references them in place.

A photograph can have several versions. A version is one development: the same file, a different rendering. Each has its own history, and you move between them without duplicating anything on disk.

A useful consequence: backing up the library folder backs up all of your editing work. The photographs themselves are wherever you put them — inside the library if you copied, on your own disk if you did not.

2. The first hour

Opening a library

On first launch Leyline creates one for you. To open another, or make a second: File ▸ Open Library…. The most recent ones are under File ▸ Open Recent. Several libraries is a legitimate choice — one per year, one per client — and they do not talk to each other.

Importing

File ▸ Import… or I. Point at a folder, then three choices:

  • Copy into library — files are filed under Photos/. Clear it to reference them in place (a memory card: copy; an already organised disk: reference).
  • Recursive — descend into subfolders.
  • Pair RAW+JPEG — the camera's JPEG is attached to its RAW rather than taking a cell of its own.

Look first lists what an import would take, writing nothing: you tick what you want, and files the library already holds are marked. Exact (reads every file) compares fingerprints rather than names: it is slower, and it is what recognises a photograph that was renamed.

Watching a folder

File ▸ Auto Import: anything that lands in the watched folder enters the library. Handy for an offload folder; the watch stops when you say so.

3. Culling

In the grid: 1…5 for stars, 0 to clear them, 6…9 for colour labels, P to pick, X to reject, U to unflag. The top bar filters on all of it, and the Shot filter adds the body, the lens, ISO, aperture, focal length and shutter speed.

Ctrl+A selects everything the grid is showing — filter included — and Ctrl+D goes back to one photograph. The line under the filters always says how many photographs a batch action would touch.

Advancing after a rating

The Advance chip at the bottom moves to the next photograph as soon as a rating is given: a run is culled without touching the arrows.

Assisted culling

Library ▸ Assisted Culling… measures the photographs and proposes rejections: bursts are grouped, the sharpest is marked, and black or blown frames are named. Every cell says why it is proposed. Nothing is written: you reject with X what you agree with, or you discard the proposal.

4. Developing

D opens develop on the selected photograph, G returns to the library, E shows the photograph large without opening develop.

The panel

On the right, the settings, grouped and foldable. Two levels: Basic shows the essentials, Full shows everything. On a slider: drag to set, hold Shift for five times finer, the wheel moves one notch, a double-click resets, and the number on the right can be typed. A group's ⟲ resets that group; Reset All returns the photograph to how it arrived.

What is saved, and when

Nothing asks you to save. An edit becomes a revision when you release the slider; successive moves of the same slider fold into one. The History panel on the left lists those revisions and says what each one changed: click one to go back to it. Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y walk the same path step by step.

Versions and presets

New version makes a second development of the same photograph. Presets keep a look: S saves the current photograph's, and you choose what it carries. Ctrl+C and Ctrl+V copy settings from one photograph onto a whole selection; Ctrl+Shift+C chooses what the copy carries.

Reprocessing

Leyline's rendering is frozen per photograph: an image developed today will give the same pixels in ten years, even as the engine improves. To let an older photograph pick up an improvement, reprocess it: R for this one, Shift+R for the library. It creates a new revision — so it is undoable, and the old one stays in the history.

5. Exporting and printing

Ctrl+E exports the selection: JPEG, PNG, TIFF, WebP or AVIF, quality, longest edge, watermark, destination folder. An export never overwrites an existing file. Export settings are kept as presets, like looks.

Ctrl+P prints the selection: paper, orientation, margins, DPI, destination ICC profile and rendering intent. File ▸ Contact Sheet… lays the selection out over one or more PDF pages, captions included.

In develop, Soft Proof simulates a destination profile on screen, with an out-of-gamut warning. The triangles at the histogram's corners (and J) show what is clipped.

6. Organising and finding

Folders: what the disk holds, as it holds it.

Collections: groupings you make. Ctrl+N creates one; B adds the selection, Shift+B removes it. A collection can also be dynamic: it keeps the current filter (rating, camera, keywords, pick flag) and updates itself.

Keywords: a tree, in the right-hand panel. A keyword Nature/Birds/Heron also answers a search for Nature.

Search: the box in the filter bar searches the filename, the keywords, the title and caption you wrote, the creator, the rights, the camera, the lens and the capture date. Diacritics are ignored, and it searches while you type.

Map: M shows geotagged photographs on a bundled basemap. Like everything else, it works with no network.

7. When something goes wrong

An offline root

If the photographs live on an external disk that is unplugged, Leyline says so: thumbnails, ratings and settings are still there, but the original pixels are not reachable. Plug the disk back in, or say where it went in File ▸ Roots….

A long job

An import, an export or a reprocess shows in the bottom right of the window, with a Cancel button. It stops between two photographs: what is done stays done.

Reporting a problem

Help ▸ Report a Problem… prepares a folder — your description, the version, the configuration, and a screenshot if you attach one. Leyline assembles it and opens it; nothing is sent: you pass it on wherever you want.

Updates

Leyline reaches the network only if you ask it to, to check that a newer version exists. Nothing about you or your photographs is transmitted, and an update always installs on a click. The choice lives in File ▸ Preferences….
