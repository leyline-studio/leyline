# Architecture Decision Records

Every structural decision is recorded here, on the same plan: **context**, **decision**, **consequences**, **alternatives rejected**. It is the last heading that gives an ADR its value — it says what was considered and then rejected, and why, which no reading of the code can reconstruct.

## How to read this folder

It is not meant to be read end to end. Three uses:

* **Understanding an external brick** — 0001 to 0005 (Rust, Slint, SQLite, LibRaw, Lensfun/LittleCMS).
* **Understanding the data model** — 0007 (a Git-inspired model), 0008 (the version as the unit), 0010 (relative paths).
* **Understanding the rendering** — 0012 (parallelism), 0028 then **0042** and **0043** (render versioning), and the pixel-feature ADRs 0013, 0016–0018, 0029–0035.

## Editing rule

**Before the first public release, an ADR stays freely editable**: an ADR that no longer matches the implementation is corrected in place, not superseded. That latitude stops on the day the project opens to the world — accepted decisions then freeze, and are replaced by new ADRs referencing them, never edited.

What never relaxes on either side of that line: **decide and document before coding**.

### The paths cited by the ADRs before 0042

The ADRs earlier than [0042](0042-versioned-stage-pipeline.md) name `processN.rs` modules — `process1.rs` to `process11.rs` — that **no longer exist**. The pipeline was reorganized into versioned stages ([0042](0042-versioned-stage-pipeline.md)) and then its pre-release history collapsed ([0043](0043-collapse-prerelease-render-history.md)): an operator that lived in a whole copy of the pipeline now lives in `crates/leyline-engine/src/stages/<operator>/vN.rs`.

Those citations are **not** corrected, and that is deliberate: they describe an act at its date — "`process6.rs`, a whole copy of `process5.rs`" tells how the module was created, and rewriting it as a path of today would make the sentence false while making it clickable. The editing rule above governs what an ADR *asserts about the present*, not what it recounts of the past.

To find the code for such an ADR: the operator it describes, under `stages/`, at the version it created.

A superseded ADR is never deleted: it stays readable, with its reasoning of the time. [ADR 0028](0028-process-version-per-feature.md) is the example — its reasoning was correct for the data it had, and it had itself foreseen its reopening.

The decision was already made one step earlier: [ADR 0065](0065-selective-import.md) §2 ("The preview comes from the file, never from the pipeline") justifies it word for word, and the only reason it gave for not keeping those previews — "the cache is indexed by asset, and these files do not have one" — disappears once the asset is created. The design point is elsewhere: a body's embedded preview **is not a revision's render**, and passing it off as one would make `valid_preview` lie forever — no render would come to replace it and an `undo` would bring back the Canon JPEG believing it was showing a development. Hence a `previews.origin` column (migration 6), carried by the row and not deduced from a path; `valid_preview` gains `AND origin = 0`, the embedded preview anchors on the initial revision and escapes [ADR 0075](0075-preview-cache-retention.md)'s retention window — it belongs to the file, not to the history. `cached_preview` returns **the best displayable image**; Studio's thumbnail timer, which already drains a `missing` queue, only changes criterion: **we pay to render what we look at, not what we import**. `ImportOptions` inherits `ScanOptions`'s `thumbnails: bool` (~20 ms/file, ×35) — an import option, not a preference ([ADR 0078](0078-preferences-panel.md) §1). The price is stated: the grid shows the **Canon** rendering on a never-visited photo, then the Leyline rendering — **the colour changes once**, which Lightroom sells under the name "Embedded & Sidecar". A preview that is missing, unreadable or **smaller than the requested class** falls back on the render: a blurry enlarged thumbnail would be worse than a slow thumbnail. Orientation was a trap already defused by `scan.rs::embedded_preview` (LibRaw does not rotate the preview, which *sometimes* carries its own EXIF tag). Open and named: the **full-resolution** JPEG decode (17.9 Mpx to produce 256 px) becomes 62 % of the pass, and `zune-jpeg` does not expose DCT-scaled decoding — a dependency decision, unmeasured. No pipeline pixel moves, §5.1 not implicated. **A RAW+JPEG companion has no thumbnail to produce** (§5): [ADR 0079](0079-raw-jpeg-pairing.md) §5 takes it out of the grid and the details panel shows only its name — no screen displays its thumbnail, and the pass produced one anyway, i.e. **half the work thrown away** on a folder set that way. Neutral by construction: with no pairs, nothing is skipped. On 29 real pairs of the corpus, ~27.5 s → **~4.6 s** (×6, of which ×2 for §5 alone). And the companion **is not a better source** for the master's thumbnail, against intuition: measured **161 ms against 119**, because it is a higher-quality encoding (5.2–10.6 MB against 1.3–3.2) hence longer to decode, for the same image. Unpairing and the explicit pairing pass fall back on the lazy path. **Revised** after review: the decision bears on the **preview path**, not on the import pass — putting it in the pass would have left the **lazy** path (the one that fills the grid as it is scrolled) paying 680 ms per visible cell, i.e. over a minute per screen, making the pass the only way to have a usable grid: the opposite of the goal. Hence §4: **import hands back control as soon as the catalog is written** (~20 ms/file) and the pass leaves as a background job — filling the catalog and filling the cache are two jobs. And it is **parallel**: the code's comment gave an exact reason not to parallelize it (serialization on the decode-cache and stage-cache mutexes, the catalog lock being already released by [ADR 0023](0023-catalog-lock-narrowing-preview.md)), and a thumbnail drawn from the embedded preview touches neither — the objection falls with its cause. **128 → 24 ms/file, ×5.3 measured** on 16 cores. On 15,000 CR2s: a usable catalog in **~5 min** instead of 2 h 50, the grid entirely warm ~6 min later in the background, and **122 ms per cell** for whoever never warms it up. The pass **follows the grid's order**, not the import's (asking for the default query's first rows costs 0.46 ms since [ADR 0081](0081-grid-page-cost.md) — the ordering is free); but **the first page is already not the problem**: `AssetsAdded` triggers `reload` → `load_window`, which partitions the missing thumbnails **visible rows first**, and Studio knows its filter and its sort where the engine does not. A screen of 100 cells fills in **~4 s** against ~23 today. **Implemented** on 2026-08-26/27 (migration 6, `previews.origin`). One trade-off was settled while writing the code, §1 and §3 contradicting each other: the embedded preview **is** the thumbnail as long as the photo is not developed — it is not a stopgap replaced as soon as one looks at the cell. The rejected branch cost 680 ms of CPU per photo scrolled past, for life, and made the colour of every thumbnail change under the user's eyes; this one makes it change only at the **first retouch**, when they expect it. A happy consequence: **Studio does not change by a line**, a client needing no notion of provenance. Measured on the real binary, 10 CR2s, warm cache: **13 ms/file** with no warm-up (against 695) and **33 ms/file** including the parallel warm-up, at 500–640 % CPU — i.e. ~3 min and ~8 min over 15,000 CR2s, against 2 h 50. |
